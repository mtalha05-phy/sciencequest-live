📐 View Mathematical Model & Equations (Advanced Learners) (Click to expand)

The system solves the one-dimensional Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation:

[-ℏ² / (2m) d²/dx² + V(x)] ψ(x) = E ψ(x)

For a rectangular barrier of height V₀ and width a with incident sub-barrier energy 0 < E < V₀, the decay parameter inside the barrier is:

κ = √(2m(V₀ - E)) / ℏ

The exact quantum transmission probability T is given analytically by:

T = [ 1 + (V₀² sinh²(κ·a)) / (4·E·(V₀ - E)) ]⁻¹

Unitarity guarantees conservation of probability:

R = 1 - T

For over-barrier energies (E > V₀), with wavevector k₂ = √(2m(E - V₀)) / ℏ:

T = [ 1 + (V₀² sin²(k₂·a)) / (4·E·(E - V₀)) ]⁻¹

And at the exact continuous boundary E = V₀:

lim_{E → V₀} T = [ 1 + (m·a²·V₀) / (2·ℏ²) ]⁻¹

Classical Mechanics vs Quantum Mechanics

Does quantum mechanics prove classical physics was "wrong"? No! Under appropriate physical conditions (high action, large mass, environmental decoherence), quantum predictions blend seamlessly into Newtonian trajectories. Test the boundaries here.

🔵 Classical Physics Paradigm

  • Deterministic Trajectories: Position x(t) and momentum p(t) are simultaneously known with arbitrary precision.
  • Rigid Energy Barriers: Kinetic energy K = E - V ≥ 0 must be non-negative everywhere.
  • Independent Particles: Particles never interfere with themselves.

🟣 Quantum Physics Paradigm

  • Wavefunctions: Systems are described by probability amplitudes ψ(x) evolving under Schrödinger's equation.
  • Evanescent Penetration: Wave amplitude decays exponentially in classically forbidden regions, producing tunneling.
  • Uncertainty Bound: Position and conjugate momentum satisfy Δx · Δp ≥ ℏ / 2.
